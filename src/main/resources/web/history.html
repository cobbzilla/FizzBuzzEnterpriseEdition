Company History

We were asked to count to one hundred. We built an institution capable of explaining why this required an institution. Progress is measured in approval chains per integer, a metric on which we have become almost impossible to outperform.

Confounding the courage to convene

Our story begins with FizzBuzzEnterpriseEdition, the magnificent enterprise Java parody created by EnterpriseQualityCoding and its contributors. They took a tiny counting exercise and gave it the architecture, indirection, and institutional gravity of a system responsible for several national economies.

They deserve all the credit for the original project and the inspiration behind this one.

We laughed. Then we made a decision.

The Serious Business Company, Inc. was incorporated inside a recurring calendar invitation whose organizer had left the company before the company existed. By its third occurrence, the invitation had a steering committee. By its fifth, the committee had determined that counting was somebody else’s responsibility.

Fortunately, somebody had an AI coding agent.

The instruction was straightforward: keep finding ways to make FizzBuzz more enterprise. Find an innocent operation. Give it interfaces. Establish authorities. Require documents. Write tests proving that the documents require the correct documents. Explain the commercial significance of the achievement. Publish it. Repeat.

We mistook an instruction without an ending for a business strategy.

A human remained in the loop, providing essential oversight through interventions such as “yes, give the number one an HR department.” The agent proposed further architecture. We approved further architecture. The resulting architecture revealed exciting opportunities for further architecture.

Somewhere along the way, “what a horribly hilarious idea” entered the minutes as “strong stakeholder support.”

One now completes recruitment and probation before it can begin counting. Every letter is formally procured. Repeated z’s carry separate receiving papers. An independent notary checks the answer. Yesterday’s answer must appear before a court to obtain permission to be used again.

We have a Certificate of Authorized Non-Performance. It takes work to obtain.

Eventually, the entire Java enterprise moved into the browser. You can now accommodate this organization on your own computer, an operating model we consider exceptionally favorable to the company.

Through all of this, fifteen has continued to be divisible by three and five. Its refusal to acknowledge our organizational development has been a source of considerable executive disappointment.

A reasonable institution might eventually ask whether the exercise had gone far enough. We have an agent continuously looking for the next improvement and a human who keeps finding its proposals funny.

Well, here we are.

Please visit the original repository. EnterpriseQualityCoding and its contributors supplied the code, the joke, and the inspiration.

We take full responsibility for this ongoing incident and promise to diligently enumerate and document all possible remediations prior to any committee decision on corrective action. Thank you for your time and attention! Please enjoy your stay in our recursive nightmare.
